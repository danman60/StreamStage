## Talk 1 — the content day

### What's actually wrong

**It's two talks wearing one title.** There's a content-day workshop (`1–237`) and there's a
recital-media-business talk (`241–291`). The second one is better. It's the one with a number in it,
the one people came to the booth about afterward (`:1243–1255`, `:1847–1863`), and the one with an
actual decision in it — *stop selling discs, charge a media fee.* It is also the one with **zero
slides**. My opinion: that's backwards. "Do the hard thing once" is a nice thesis but it's a
content-marketing thesis; "you are losing money on your recital video and here's the fix" is a
business thesis, and this was the Business track.

**The deck ran out before the talk did.** It was built for ~20 minutes; he delivered ~60. He wasn't
over-running — he was under-planned by 3×, and from roughly the media-fee block onward he was flying
without instruments. That is exactly where the filler rate spikes and where he says it himself:
*"I want to have some time for questions. I'm kind of like rambling a lot here"* `:359`. The fix is
not "talk less." The fix is that the last 40 minutes need slides too.

**He never said the call to action.** `streamstage.live/book` appears zero times in the transcript.
The whole planned close (`talk1-video-slides.md:130`) did not happen. What he actually said was
"send me an email" `:343` and "I'm Daniel, come talk to me" `:411`. My opinion: warm, and it cost
him bookings. A room of 60 studio owners at the end of a good talk is the highest-intent moment he
will get all year, and he ended it with a vibe instead of an instruction.

**Station 4 fell out of his own list** and the audience had to ask for it — *"What was the fourth
station?"* `:225`, and his answer opens *"Extra. Yeah, extra. Thank you. I was like, I don't know"*
`:229`. The four stations are the spine of the talk. Losing one in front of the room isn't a
memory problem, it's a structure problem: they were never on screen as a thing he had to click
through one at a time.

**Three failed media playbacks in a row** at `:133–153` — *"hold on, I have another thing running"*,
*"Should we listen to it?"*, *"Is there a bit audio on this one? No, okay, thanks for playing."*
That's at minute ~10, at the exact beat where the reel is supposed to do the selling. My opinion:
this is the worst moment in either talk, worse than the two broken demos in talk 2, because in talk
2 the failures were interesting and here it was just dead air.

**The StudioSage teaser broke his own guardrail and I think the guardrail was right.**
`talk1-runofshow.md:30` says no StudioSage content in talk 1. He did ~7 minutes of it, `:299–327`,
with the price, the mechanic, the dashboard, the whole reveal. It also produced the only tagged
laugh in the talk (`(Audience Laughing)` `:315`, the snark slider). So: the *instinct* was right,
the *dose* was wrong. Seven minutes is a second talk given badly. Thirty seconds is a trailer. Keep
the snark-slider line, cut everything else.

### What's genuinely good and should be protected

The **provoke technique** `:13` is the best sixty seconds of teaching in either talk. It's specific,
it's counterintuitive, it changes what someone does on Tuesday, and it has a worked example built in.
It deserves a slide and it now has a handout.

The **urine-sample analogy** — I know he was second-guessing it live (*"why am I saying this"* `:17`,
*"there's no notes here about the urine sample"* `:37`). My opinion: keep it. It's the only line in
the talk anyone will still be able to repeat in six months, and it does real work — it encodes
"cut the top and tail" in a way nobody forgets. But **commit to it or cut it.** Telling a slightly
rude joke and then apologising for it twice is the worst of both. Say it once, land it, move on.

The **questions the room asked were good ones** — hero video `:201`, UGC vs pro `:205`, distribution
across platforms `:213`, aspect ratios `:371`, frame rate `:375`. Specific questions are the sign of
a talk that's landing. That's not a Q&A to shorten; it's evidence the content-day material works.

**"The world is healing"** after the DVD hands-up `:245`. Best throwaway line in the talk.

### My recommendation, stated plainly

Rebuild talk 1 as a **real 60 minutes in three acts**: the content day (the method) → the media fee
(the money) → what you walk out with (the payoff), with the StudioSage teaser as a 30-second sting
between acts two and three. The media-fee act is what makes it a Business-track talk instead of a
video-nerd talk.

## Talk 2 — AI

### What's actually wrong

**Twelve minutes before the first free move.** `2565–2673` is title, robot camera, Oprah, who-I-am,
roles arc, push/pull, room gauge, the promise — and *then* the facelift plant, *then* history, *then*
the 101 section. The first actual free move (`talk, don't type`) lands around `:2745`. He promised
five free moves; the room waited a fifth of the hour for the first one. My opinion: cut to move one
by minute six. The 101 material can be distributed *between* the moves instead of front-loaded, and
most of the roles arc is redundant with the front-desk slide.

**Two live beats, both failed.** SMS demo `:2939–2983`, facelift reveal `:3011–3043`. He handled it
about as well as it can be handled — *"those are the two live parts of my demo that crashed and
burned. I told you it was going to be very exciting"* `:3043` — and the room stayed with him. But my
opinion is unambiguous: **never run two live beats in one talk.** One is a brave moment; two is a
pattern, and the second failure retroactively reframes the first one as "this guy's stuff doesn't
work." His own words at the booth after: *"it's like the live demo thing was unfortunate, right? But
people were taking pictures, they were screenshot like everything"* `:3439`.

If one survives, keep the **SMS demo** and cut the facelift reveal. The facelift depends on an
external API and a 60–90 minute background job started at minute 11 — it is the most fragile thing
in the hour and its payoff is a website for one person the rest of the room doesn't know. The SMS
demo is the product.

**And change how the SMS demo runs.** He demoed it *at* the room — forwarded one email himself,
`:2939–2951`. My strong opinion: have the *room* do it. Read the number, put it on screen, get forty
phones texting it at once. Then it cannot fail — if three texts hang, thirty-seven landed, and the
person next to you shows you theirs. You also get the room's real questions instead of your test
question, and every phone that texts is a lead. The run-of-show actually deferred "text it now" to
slide 33 (`talk2-runofshow.md:65`); that's too late.

**The pricing message contradicts itself.** "$20 a month" `:2903`, then "free until 2027" `:2907`,
then "$20/month for anybody who signs up in September" `:3059`, then "free for you until 2027, then
$20 a month" `:3067`. My opinion: the free-until-2027 kills the $20. If it's free for eighteen
months, nobody is evaluating it at $20 — they're evaluating whether it's worth ten minutes of setup.
Lead with the value and the setup cost, mention the price once, flatly, at the end.

**"Does that make sense?" four times**, all clustered in the demo Q&A `:2907–2915`, all while
explaining what StudioSage *can't* scope to a specific dancer. That's not a verbal tic, that's the
sound of being asked something the deck has no slide for — twice, by two different people
(`:2907`, `:3079`). It needs an honest limits slide.

**The close kept going.** He put the CTA up, delivered a good landing (*"I've been Daniel, come talk
to me"*), and then said *"ask me questions right now, it's not over, go"* `:3067` and ran another
several minutes. Good instinct, wrong order. My opinion: take questions *before* the CTA, then close
and stop. The last thing the room hears should be the thing you want them to do.

### What's genuinely good and should be protected

**The Oprah open** `:2601–2605` is better than the planned one. It's current, it's a name everyone
knows, it makes AI feel adjacent rather than alien, and it arrives at a real question about human
interaction. My opinion: make it the actual cold open and retire the Sheridan/"you will not see me
dance" bit.

**"AI is like Kleenex"** `:2673` does more useful work in one sentence than the whole 101 matrix
slide does.

**"$10,000 an hour"** `:2829` — *what's the highest-leverage thing you can be doing? Talking to your
dance families.* That is the actual thesis of the hour and it's stronger than the planned
"what's your time worth" close, because it answers the question instead of asking it.

**"If you're drafting an email six or seven times, pick up the phone"** `:2765`, `:2769`. Said twice,
unprompted. The AI guy telling a room not to use AI is the most credible thing he says all hour.

**The two audience-supplied tips** — the costume-design → image → Canadian no-tariff supplier chain
`:2963`, and "strict adherence, you may not change the picture" `:2817`. Both better and more
studio-specific than anything on a slide, both free, both from the room. My opinion: stop hoping for
these and build a deliberate two-minute "what's working for you?" slot. It costs two minutes and it's
the only part of the talk that can't be gotten from YouTube.

**Nineteen direct asks to the room in an hour.** That's the real reason talk 2 worked. Whatever else
changes, don't lose that.

### My recommendation, stated plainly

Talk 2's problem is compression, not content. It carried ~93 minutes into 60 and he cut live, on his
feet, in real time — which is why Routine 436, the poster beat and the dashboard walkthrough
vanished. Rebuild it *at 60 minutes on paper* so the cuts are decisions instead of casualties, move
to the first free move by minute six, and run exactly one live beat with the room's phones instead
of his.
