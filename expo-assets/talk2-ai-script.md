## A VERY SHORT HISTORY (0:14–0:16) · Slide 8

For fifty years, computers made **you** learn **their** language. Commands. Exact file names. The
right button in the right menu. One character wrong and it just says no. Everybody in this room has
felt stupid in front of a computer, and it was never your fault.

Then they started learning **ours**, one sense at a time. Spellcheck. Autocorrect. Your phone finding
your daughter's face in four thousand photos. "Okay Google, take me home." Nobody called that AI. It
just stopped making you do the work.

And now they've learned the whole thing: **plain English**. The most powerful software ever built
ships with a text box and no manual. The breakthrough isn't the intelligence — **it's the interface.**

**[CLICK]** You feel like you're behind. **You are not behind.** This is the biggest shift since the
internet — since the printing press. We are all very new at it.

**[CLICK]** And by the way: 99% of the time somebody says "AI", they mean **ChatGPT, Claude or
Perplexity**. That's the whole vocabulary.

*Pivot:* which means the fastest way in isn't typing carefully — it's talking.

## 101 — WHAT IS THIS THING? (0:16–0:19) · Slide 9

It read almost everything ever written, and it predicts what comes next. That's it. Then it learned

**The magic:** anything made of language. Write it, rewrite it, shorten it, sort it, put it in your

**The catch:** it can be **confidently wrong**.

**[CLICK]** My favourite: you ask it, *"should I walk to the car wash or drive? It's only ten minutes

**[CLICK]** So: **trust, but verify.** Check it before you hit send. You are the editor, always.
*(Backup joke if the room is flat: ask it how many R's are in "strawberry".)*

## 101 — THE ONLY SEVEN WORDS (0:22–0:25) · Slide 11 — the pyramid

## MOVE 1 — Talk, don't type (0:25–0:30) · Slide 12

I've been working with this stuff for about a year and a half, same as everybody. The single biggest
thing that sped me up wasn't a model or a plugin. It was a button: **the microphone.**

We have something in common — studio owners love to talk. And you think differently when you talk than
when you type. I type fast, I play piano, and I *still* talk to my robots all day, because things come
out mid-sentence that never would have made it through my fingers.

To be clear: not voice *mode*, where it talks back. **I read faster than the robot talks** — that's for
driving. I mean **voice typing**: the little mic on your phone keyboard, the dictation key on your
laptop.

**[LIVE / CLICK ×4]** Watch. *(Hit the mic and ramble a real Monday:)* "Okay so costume payments are
late again and I need to remind parents without sounding annoyed for the third time… and Tuesday
ballet isn't filling like Thursday tap… book the photographer, call StreamStage about video… and I
need a poster, fun and vibrant, warm and welcoming." **Ramble. Ramble. Ramble.**

Same trick, bigger: pull your **Zoom transcripts** off zoom.us and paste them in. **Hit record** on the
teacher meeting. Record yourself in the car. People will sell you a little AI note pin — you already
own a phone.

**MONDAY:** app on your first screen, mic every time.

## MOVE 2 — Your studio in two files (0:30–0:35) · Slide 13

Here's the problem with AI writing: it sounds like nobody.

So give it two files. **A voice file** — how you talk, how you sign off, how you handle a sensitive
email, the words you use and the words you don't. *(Always **dancers** — never "kids".)* And **tell it
to stop using em dashes.** That one line fixes the most obvious tell there is.

**A brand file** — your colours, your fonts, your logo rules, the feel: warm, vibrant, welcoming. What
your studio is, and what it isn't.

These are plain text one-pagers **you own**. Not a setting inside somebody's app. ChatGPT today,
Claude next month, whatever exists in 2031 — the value is in **your** files.

How do you fill the voice file honestly? If you're on Gmail, **Google Takeout** exports all of your
email. Hand it the real thing. Or drop fifty old newsletters into NotebookLM — it's free, it's
Google's, you upload a pile of documents and it builds a knowledge base out of them — and ask, *"what
patterns do you see in how I write?"* Then the monthly studio email is: *"here's what we have — pajama
day Friday, the twelve-year-old Acro hair moved from a messy bun to a high pony"* — and out it comes,
in your voice. Ten minutes instead of two hours.

**MONDAY:** start both files. One page each. Boring is fine.

## MOVE 3 — Make it write the prompt (0:37–0:42) · Slide 15

Everybody's real problem is *"I don't know what to type."*

So stop writing the prompt. **Ask it to write the prompt.**

**[CLICK]** *"I need a poster for our June recital. Write me the prompt to make that image — ask me

**[CLICK]** And it hands you back the careful version: vertical 18×24, deep plum, soft warm spotlight

**[CLICK]** You could never have typed that. You didn't have to. You're not the prompt engineer — it
is. You just edit **its** prompt. That's the entire skill.

Related habit: ask **how** questions, not just do-this questions. *"How would I go about doing this?"*
Half the time you don't know what "this" even is yet.

**[CLICK]** And it writes **your two files** as well: *"read my website and my last ten newsletters,
then write me a brand file and a voice file."* You hand-author nothing. You just correct it.

**MONDAY:** next bad answer — go fix the *prompt*, not the answer.

## WHERE IT SHOULD NOT REPLACE YOU (0:53–0:55) · Slide 19 — ⚠ published promise, do not cut
