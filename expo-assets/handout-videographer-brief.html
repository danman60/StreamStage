Most recital video goes wrong in the booking conversation, not on the night.
Give this to whoever is shooting your recital — a local videographer, a company, or a parent with a good camera. You don't need to understand it; they will. It's all normal to ask for, and if someone says it isn't, that's useful information about them.

Coverage — what we cannot go back for

  • One clean, centred wide of the whole stage, running continuously — the safety master. Everything else is a bonus.
  • At least one closer angle for faces. A recital with no faces in it is a recording, not a film.
  • Shoot the room, not just the stage — audience, applause, a parent's reaction, the hallway afterward, and the lobby and backstage: hair, nerves, the huddle before the entrance.
  • Vertical clips grabbed deliberately. Not cropped out of the landscape master later.
  • Open gate if the camera does it (the near-square sensor mode) — that's what makes one shot into both a landscape cut and a full-resolution vertical. 4K, 30fps unless they give you a reason, and a reason is fine.

Audio — the half everyone forgets

  • A tested feed from the house board for music, mics and announcements.
  • Plus a room or audience mic, so applause survives the edit. A board feed alone sounds sterile, a room mic alone sounds distant — you want both.
  • Monitored together before doors open, not discovered afterward.

Backup — non-negotiable

  • Cards backed up twice, in two places, before anyone leaves the building.
  • A local recording even if it's being livestreamed. The local file is the archive; the stream is the convenience.

Delivery — where recital video actually gets judged SPEED AND EASE BEAT PICTURE QUALITY, ONCE QUALITY IS ACCEPTABLE

  • A permanent digital link, not a disc and not a USB key. Families lose the physical thing, and a scratch kills it.
  • A gallery split per routine, with thumbnails and correct titles — studio, show, date, routine, teacher. Titles are where recital videos go wrong.
  • Downloadable, not stream-only, so families keep their own copy — and every routine and teacher name spelled correctly: send the running order in writing and hold them to it.
  • A stated delivery date, in days not seasons. Ten days is achievable; September is not acceptable for a June show.
  • A highlight reel, vertical social cuts, and next season's registration film — ask up front; they cost almost nothing at booking and a lot afterward.

The money model — charge a media fee, stop selling discs

Nobody buys media by the object any more. Families are paying for the media to be created — so stop selling copies at the door and put a media fee on every dancer, the way you already handle costumes.

Ask your videographer: "what's your per-dancer rate?" → set your media fee above it → every family gets the link, you never chase a sale, and you never wonder whether the video made money. Your fee minus their rate, times your dancer count, is your margin — decided in advance, in writing.

Five questions to ask before you sign

  1. What's your turnaround, in days, from show night to the parent link? — an evasive answer here is the whole answer
  2. How do families receive it, who hosts it, and for how long? — "we'll send a link" is not a plan
  3. Open gate, or how are you handling vertical? — social content, or just a show recording
  4. Per-dancer rate, and what's included at it? — reels, titles, backups, angles: named, not assumed
  5. Board feed and a room mic? — fastest way to learn if they've done this before

Recital night is peak vibes — everyone dressed up, everyone in the building, once.
