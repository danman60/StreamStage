# What the Toronto follow-ups actually did — measured, not remembered

Source: Gmail sent mail, 2026-07-28 → 2026-08-09. 82 sent messages pulled, 59 threads,
40 of them expo-lead threads (existing clients, vendors and personal mail excluded).
Toronto Dance Teacher Expo was **Wed 2026-07-29**; day offsets below are from that date.

This exists so the Calgary campaign repeats what worked instead of being invented.

---

## 1. The cadence you actually ran

| Touch | When | What it was |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **+1 day** (Jul 30) | Personal email, one per studio, written from what they said at the booth |
| 2 | **+5 to +7 days** (Aug 4–5) | Reply **on the same thread**, same subject line, shorter |
| 3 | **+8 to +9 days** (Aug 6–7) | Only for some; usually a new angle or an answer to a reply |

Nothing went out on expo day itself, and nothing went out after +9. The busiest single
window was Jul 30 10:10–11:59 — fourteen emails in under two hours, all hand-written.

**The bump is a reply in the thread, not a new email.** Every second touch reuses the
original subject, so it lands under the first one rather than starting a fresh conversation.

---

## 2. Subject lines — never generic, always their thing

What you sent:

- `Recital delivery for your 150 families`
- `Recital capture for SBS Dance`
- `Promo video, and an ideal date: Monday November 2`
- `Content day at Revolutions`
- `Studio promo film for That's Dancing`
- `That recital video checklist` · `That 5 Free AI Moves handout`
- `Your website rebuild from the Expo`
- `From the Toronto Dance Teacher Expo`

Two shapes only: **their problem in their words**, or **the thing they physically took
from the booth**. Not one "Following up", "Checking in", or "Great to meet you".

---

## 3. The body, touch 1 — five parts, in this order

1. **Where you met, dated.** *"Great meeting you at the Toronto Dance Teacher Expo on
   Wednesday, and thanks for coming to the talk."*
2. **A callback only they would recognise.** Their videographer retired · 150 families ·
   they run karate and dance · they travelled a long way · they put their studio on screen
   for the live rebuild. This is the line that proves it is not a blast.
3. **The value sentence**, near-verbatim across most emails:
   > *"painless on your side, excellent quality for your families, and an easy margin for
   > the studio on hiring us"*
   and its concrete half: *"every family gets their own dancer's routines delivered straight
   to them. No USB sticks, no chasing download links, nothing landing back on your desk."*
4. **Something free, already made, no strings.** The rebuilt website, live on a URL:
   *"That is yours to keep either way, no strings and nothing owed."* Or when it is
   conditional, said plainly: *"included if we work together."*
5. **One CTA, the same one every time**: `https://meet.streamstage.live/book/intro-call`

Sign-off drifted: the +1 emails carry the long corporate block (twice over, duplicated
signature), the later ones a short `Daniel / StreamStage · 647-883-3307`. The short one
reads better and is what the later, better-performing emails used.

## 4. The body, touch 2 — shorter, one new idea

Opens *"Quick idea for you on the ___"*, restates the value in one line, adds the free
website link if it was not already sent, same booking CTA. No "just checking in", no
apology for following up, no recap of the first email.

---

## 5. What got replies

**14 of 40 expo threads replied — 35%.** By type:

| Email type | Replied |
|---|---|
| `5 Free AI Moves handout` (the freebie they took) | **3 of 4** |
| Named-problem subject (`150 families`, `SBS Dance`, `That's Dancing`) | 4 of 6 |
| Concrete date + a reason (`ideal date: Monday November 2`, travel fee waived) | 3 of 3 |
| `That recital video checklist` | **0 of 5** |
| `Your website rebuild from the Expo` | 0 of 1 |
| `Two things from the Expo` | 0 of 1 |
| `Your StudioSage sign-in link` (support) | 0 of 2 |

### The three findings worth acting on

1. **A concrete date beats an open invitation.** Muskoka got *"I have an ideal date for you:
   Monday November 2. I'm already going to be up your way that week, so if we shoot then I
   can waive the travel fee entirely"* — and a reason it suits their calendar (after tickets
   open Oct 15, five weeks before the Dec 6 show). It replied. "Grab a time whenever suits"
   mostly did not.
2. **The freebie email outperformed everything.** The people who took the AI moves handout
   replied at the highest rate of any group, and that email is the one that names a price
   plainly (*"It's $20 a month normally. Right now you're in free"*) and offers an out
   (*"No problem at all if it's not the right time"*).
3. **The free website, on its own, did not pull.** `Your website rebuild from the Expo` got
   no reply, and the checklist emails that carried a rebuilt-site link got none either. It
   works as a **proof point inside** an email about their problem, not as the subject of one.

### Caveats — do not overread this

- Small sample: 40 threads, and some studios appear as several threads because the same
  email was sent three times (Muskoka).
- **A silent thread is not a failed email.** Replies by phone, text, or straight through the
  booking link are invisible here. That's Dancing shows 2 inbound and went on to a booked
  intro call and a contract; Urban Dance replied on the AI moves thread.
- Open and click rates do not exist for these — they were sent from Gmail, not a platform.
  Reply is the only signal available, so it is the only one used.

## 6. What this means for Calgary

The shape to copy, unchanged:

- **+1 day**: one email per lead, subject = their problem or the thing they took, body =
  where-we-met → their own words back → the value sentence → something free already built →
  the booking link.
- **+5 days**: reply on the same thread, "Quick idea for you on ___", one new idea.
- **+8 days**: only where there is a genuine new reason — a date you will be in their area,
  their recital month approaching, an answer to something they asked.

The two upgrades the data argues for:

- **Put a real date in touch 1 wherever you can.** Calgary is a flight, so "I'm out west the
  week of X" is worth more there than it was in Ontario.
- **Lead with the freebie they took**, not with the website rebuild. The rebuild belongs in
  paragraph four as proof, exactly where it worked.

Still unanswered and needed before anything automated goes out: how many touches you want
automated versus hand-written, and whether the booth's own instant delivery email
(`lead-assets.ts`, subject `<asset> — as promised`) counts as touch 0. Today that email is
the ONLY automatic thing; every follow-up above was typed by hand.
